# VP-100 Centrifugal Pump — Owner's Manual

## Overview
The Vardhan VP-100 is a single-stage centrifugal monoblock pump for domestic water supply, small farms and light industrial transfer. It is designed for clean water up to 45°C. Serial numbers start with `VP100-`.

## Specifications
- Motor: 1 HP (0.75 kW), single phase, 230 V, 50 Hz
- Maximum head: 32 metres
- Maximum flow: 4,200 litres per hour
- Suction lift: up to 7 metres
- Pipe size: 25 mm x 25 mm (1 inch)
- Recommended fuse/MCB: 10 A

## Installation
Mount the pump on a level concrete base, as close to the water source as possible. Keep the suction pipe short and always sloping upward towards the pump, with no air pockets. Fit a foot valve with a strainer at the end of the suction pipe. Use a voltage stabiliser if your supply drops below 200 V.

## Priming and first start
The VP-100 is not self-priming. Before the first start, remove the priming plug and fill the pump casing and suction pipe completely with water. Close the plug and start the pump. Never run the pump dry, even for a few seconds: dry running damages the mechanical seal within minutes.

## Troubleshooting: unusual noise or vibration
A rattling or "gravel" sound usually means cavitation: the pump is starving for water. Check that the foot valve strainer is not blocked, that the suction pipe has no air leaks, and that the suction lift is under 7 metres. A humming noise without the shaft turning means a stuck impeller or a failed capacitor; switch off immediately and call service. Vibration usually comes from loose mounting bolts; tighten all four base bolts.

## Troubleshooting: low pressure or low flow
Check for a partly closed valve on the delivery side, a clogged foot valve, or air in the suction line. Re-prime the pump. If the impeller is worn (pumps older than 5 years on sandy water), it must be replaced by a service technician.

## Troubleshooting: motor overheating or tripping
The thermal overload trips when the motor gets too hot. Common causes are low voltage (below 200 V), running against a closed valve for a long time, or poor ventilation around the motor. Let the motor cool for 30 minutes before restarting. If it trips again within an hour, book a service visit.

## Troubleshooting: water leakage
A small drip from the shaft area points to a worn mechanical seal. Seals are a wear part and should be replaced every 2 to 3 years in normal use.

## Maintenance schedule
- Every month: clean the foot valve strainer; check for leaks.
- Every 6 months: check the base bolts and the electrical connections.
- Every 2 years: replace the mechanical seal (service visit recommended).
